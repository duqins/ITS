# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Project Concept

Universities regularly purchase furniture, computers, electronic devices, laboratory equipment, books and office materials while similar usable items sit unused, stored or discarded in other departments. The problem is rarely a shortage of reusable resources; it is the absence of an integrated system that can identify, classify, match, transfer, maintain and track those resources throughout their life cycle.

This project designs and prototypes an internal campus platform, the Intelligent, AI-Powered Circular Campus Resource Exchange and Asset Life Cycle Management System, that combines a classic, rule-based asset-management core (registration, marketplace, requests, approvals, transfers, maintenance, role-based access, reports) with AI-enhanced services (semantic matching, automatic classification, sustainable-action recommendations, an LLM assistant and generative reporting). The system is built in eight phases over the Fall 2026 semester and gives the team hands-on experience of the full software development life cycle while comparing traditional and AI-augmented engineering approaches.

## 1.2 Document Purpose

This document is the Phase 1 deliverable. It establishes the foundation for all later phases by:

- defining the project problem, scope, objectives, assumptions and expected benefits;
- identifying all stakeholders and mapping them to system features, data needs, permissions, responsibilities and approval decisions;
- describing the current (as-is) process for surplus identification, resource requests, transfer approval, maintenance recording and disposal;
- describing the requirements-gathering methodology, summarising and prioritising the gathered requirements, and giving an initial requirements specification;
- defining measurable success criteria; and
- presenting a preliminary project plan with timeline, milestones, methodology, team structure, task assignments, dependencies, risks and resource allocation.

The document will be refined by the Feasibility Study (Phase 2) and superseded in detail by the Requirements Document (Phase 3). It is written for the lab instructors (client), the development team and future readers of the project repository.
