# 7. Measurable Project Success Criteria
The criteria below define proposed targets for evaluating the completed prototype and the team's development process. They are not results already achieved or numerical thresholds prescribed by the lab slides. The team will confirm their feasibility and acceptance with the client during Phase 3. Requirement IDs refer to Section 6.

Testing will use synthetic data containing 500 assets across 10 departments and 50 requests, including a fixed evaluation set of 20 requests for which suitable assets exist. Before evaluating matching quality, two team members will independently label suitable assets using a shared checklist covering purpose, specifications, quantity, condition, location, and required date, then resolve disagreements. Classic search and AI matching will use the same requests and asset snapshot.

The user study will involve five consenting volunteers or classmates acting in the relevant roles. Its results will provide prototype feedback rather than evidence of campus-wide acceptance.

| Area | Criterion | Target (prototype) | How measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional performance | Required workflows work from start to finish. | 100% of agreed acceptance tests for Must-have requirements pass, including registration, publication, request, reservation, approval, transfer, maintenance, and end-of-life records. | Map every Must-have FR, AI, SU, and NFR ID to at least one acceptance test; record steps, expected results, actual results, and evidence. |
| Functional performance | Classic search responds promptly (NFR-02). | At least 95 of 100 searches finish within 2 seconds with 500 assets and five concurrent users. | Time search and filtering requests; record the machine, browser, dataset, and configuration. Measure AI latency separately. |
| User acceptance | Users complete the main tasks and find the interface usable (NFR-01). | At least 4 of 5 users complete each of asset registration, finding a listing, and request submission without help; mean satisfaction is at least 4/5. | Observe role-appropriate tasks using the same task sheet; record completion, assistance, and post-task ratings. |
| Match usefulness | AI produces useful recommendations (AI-02 to AI-05). | At least 16 of 20 eligible requests have a reviewer-labelled useful asset in the top three results. | Evaluate the fixed, labelled request set. Count a request as successful when at least one top-three result is useful; failed AI results count as unsuccessful. |
| Match usefulness | Semantic matching adds value over keyword search. | AI succeeds on at least 2 more of the same 20 requests than the classic top-three baseline. | Compare top-three success counts using identical eligibility filters; include differently worded but equivalent requests and report failures as well as gains. |
| Match usefulness | Rankings respect mandatory constraints (AI-03). | Zero ineligible assets appear as actionable recommendations in the 20-request evaluation set. | Check recommended candidates against availability, technical compatibility, required quantity, date, and access rules. |
| AI classification and assistant | Suggestions and request submission remain reviewable (AI-01, AI-07, AI-08). | At least 16 of 20 labelled assets receive a correct category suggestion; when the Should-have assistant is implemented, all 5 request-submission scenarios show proposed fields and require confirmation. | Compare suggestions with the fixed labels; test confirmed, corrected, cancelled, unauthorised, and incomplete assistant requests. |
| Avoided purchases | Reuse can fulfil demand that would otherwise require a purchase (SU-03, SU-04). | At least 5 of 10 scripted purchase-need scenarios end with an approved, completed reuse transaction and a documented avoided-purchase record. | Inspect the request, receipt, reviewer confirmation, replacement-cost estimate, and attributable costs. Report these as simulated outcomes. |
| Reuse / repair / sustainability | The prototype demonstrates reuse and repair outcomes (SU-05, SU-06). | At least 5 of a fixed cohort of 10 individually tracked reusable assets return to use, giving a reuse rate of at least 50%; at least 3 separate repair cases record return to service and estimated life extension. | Trace completed transfers and repairs to the predefined cohort and status history; verify the reuse denominator and retirement-date assumptions. |
| Reuse / repair / sustainability | All required indicators are available and traceable (SU-01 to SU-08). | All 6 indicators are displayed: waste diversion, avoided purchases, reuse rate, asset-life extension, savings, and estimated carbon reductions. | Inspect dashboards and reports for the same period and confirm supporting records, units, formulas, and assumptions. |
| Reuse / repair / sustainability | Sustainability calculations are correct and handle missing data honestly. | All 10 hand-calculated cases agree at the documented rounding precision, with no duplicate benefits; missing required inputs produce N/A or an explicitly labelled estimate. | Include repeated events, zero denominators, missing mass or carbon factors, intervention costs, and negative net-benefit cases. |
| Responsible AI | People retain control over consequential actions (AI-08, AI-11). | Zero actions bypass required human confirmation or approval in 14 tests: one unauthorised and one authorised path for each of transfer, donation, recycling, disposal, repair spending, financial-value changes, and user-role changes. | Attempt each action through the relevant classic and AI entry points where supported; inspect saved state, approvals, and audit records. |
| Responsible AI | The system continues working when AI fails (AI-13, NFR-07). | All 5 tested failure conditions expose the relevant classic alternative within 15 seconds without losing entered data. | Simulate unavailable service, timeout, rate limit, malformed output, and insufficient confidence; complete the task using classic controls. |
| Responsible AI | Recommendations and generated reports are explainable (AI-04, AI-10, AI-12). | All 20 sampled recommendations show reasons and confidence information or its unavailability; all factual claims in 5 generated summaries are traceable and all reported totals match conventional reports. | Review source links, model records, assumptions, and totals; flag unsupported claims and require review before official use. |
| Security and integrity | Permissions and stock quantities remain correct (NFR-03 to NFR-05, NFR-10). | All agreed role-and-permission tests pass; in 10 two-user attempts to reserve the same single unit, exactly one reservation succeeds each time. | Test unauthorised access, restricted records, audit changes, invalid quantities and transitions, and simultaneous reservations. |
| Fairness / ethics | Irrelevant identity changes do not affect matching (NFR-12). | All 10 paired cases preserve eligibility and relative ranking when only names or department labels change and relevant policy and matching factors stay equal. | Use the same model, configuration, and controlled inputs for each pair; investigate and record differences. |
| Portability | A reviewer can run the project from the repository (NFR-09). | A teammate completes 1 fresh setup using only the README and documented prerequisites; the classic baseline works without an AI API key. | Use a clean project copy, seed the database, start the application, and complete a search and request workflow; record setup issues. |
| Deployment | The application is hosted and accessible to intended users and reviewers (NFR-13). | Before the final demonstration, one teammate completes a fresh deployment using the documented steps; the hosted registration, search, request, and approval workflows pass, including an AI-unavailable fallback check. | Record the deployed version, hosting configuration, HTTPS URL, deployment and restart steps, role-access checks, and workflow results. |
| Scalability | The Should-have growth target is evaluated (NFR-11). | At least 95 of 100 classic searches finish within 3 seconds with 2,000 assets, 200 requests, and five concurrent users. | Repeat the documented search test with the larger dataset; report this result separately from Must-have acceptance. |
| Process | Each member contributes regularly through their own GitHub account. | Proposed team target: at least 2 meaningful commits per member per active project week; for Phase 1, at least 1 meaningful commit for each of the member's 3 assigned sections. | Review authors, dates, changed content, and messages weekly. Empty, cosmetic, or artificially split commits do not count toward the team target. |
| Documentation | All phases and required evidence are delivered. | All required deliverables for Phases 1–8, including the combined Phases 4 and 5 design deliverable, are submitted by the confirmed course deadlines. | Check submission receipts, phase files, requirement-to-test links, and classic-versus-AI reflections against the course checklist. |

During testing, the team will record the achieved value, supporting evidence, and any unmet target. Synthetic avoided purchases, estimated life extension, and estimated carbon reductions will be reported as prototype demonstrations, without claiming that real purchases were prevented or real campus emissions were measured.
