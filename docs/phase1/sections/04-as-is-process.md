# 4. Study of the Current (As-Is) Process

Because the team has no access to Khalifa University's internal systems, the as-is process below was reconstructed from the project description, publicly available university asset-management practice, and a structured walkthrough exercise in the lab. It will be validated through stakeholder interviews in Phase 2 and Phase 3 (Section 5.1).

<!-- widths: 2000,3800,3560 -->
| Process | Current practice (typical) | Pain points the system must address |
|---|---|---|
| Identifying surplus assets | A department notices idle equipment during moves, audits or storage clean-outs; sometimes recorded in a spreadsheet or emailed to facilities; often not recorded at all. | No campus-wide visibility; items sit in storage; no consistent classification or condition record. |
| Requesting resources | Staff raise a purchase request through procurement; occasionally ask colleagues informally whether spare equipment exists. | Reuse depends on personal networks; procurement cannot check for existing surplus; requests lack structured specifications. |
| Approving transfers | Ad-hoc email approval between department heads; asset register updated (if at all) by the custodian afterwards. | No standard workflow, no audit trail, custody often left on the original department, location data becomes stale. |
| Recording maintenance | Maintenance requests raised through a facilities ticket or directly with a technician; repair costs tracked separately in finance. | Maintenance history not linked to the asset record; no basis for repair-vs-replace decisions. |
| Donation, recycling, disposal | Disposal committee reviews write-off lists periodically; items may be scrapped although another department could use them. | Disposal is the default; no sustainability assessment; no measurement of waste diversion or carbon impact. |
| Reporting | Fixed asset register exports; manual spreadsheets for audits. | No reuse, savings or sustainability metrics; no trend analysis or management insight. |

Target (to-be) process: every asset has one record and one custodian; surplus is published to a campus marketplace; requests are matched semantically and ranked; approvals follow a defined workflow with an audit trail; maintenance and end-of-life events are recorded against the asset; AI recommends sustainable actions and generates reports; humans approve all high-impact decisions.
