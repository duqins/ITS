# 4. Study of the Current (As-Is) Process

The team does not have access to Khalifa University's real asset-management systems or internal procedures. Therefore, this section is a preliminary view based on the project description, common university practices, and the team's walkthrough of the process. It will be checked and corrected through interviews and workshops in Phases 2 and 3.


| Process | Current practice (typical) | Main problems to solve |
|---|---|---|
| Identifying surplus assets | A department notices an unused or unwanted asset and records it in a spreadsheet, email, or local file. Other departments may only learn about it by asking around. | No campus-wide visibility, incomplete records, outdated information, and unnecessary purchases. |
| Requesting resources | Staff ask other departments by email, telephone, or chat. If nothing is found quickly, they may start a purchase request. | Requests are inconsistent, difficult to search, and not connected to available assets. |
| Approving transfers | Releasing and receiving departments exchange emails and documents. Finance or procurement may be involved when required. | Slow approvals, unclear responsibility, missing evidence, and delayed custody or location updates. |
| Recording maintenance | Defects, inspections, repairs, and costs may be stored in separate tickets, emails, invoices, or spreadsheets. | No complete maintenance history, repeated work, and weak repair-versus-replace decisions. |
| Donation, recycling, or disposal | A department requests approval and the responsible offices decide the appropriate action. External organisations may collect the asset. | Reuse or repair may be skipped, special checks may be missed, and disposal evidence may be incomplete. |
| Reporting | Staff manually combine information from inventory, finance, maintenance, and approval records. | Reports take time, figures may be inconsistent, and savings or sustainability benefits are difficult to measure. |

The main issue is that information is spread across different people, messages, and files. There is no single record connecting the asset, request, approval, transfer, maintenance, and final outcome.

In the proposed process, an authorised user registers an asset and publishes it when it becomes available. Another department searches or submits a request, and the system recommends suitable matches. The required people approve the reservation and transfer, after which ownership, custody, location, condition, collection or delivery, and receipt are recorded. Maintenance and end-of-life actions are added to the same asset history. Reuse and repair are considered before recycling or disposal, and AI recommendations never replace human approval.

The prototype will not connect to the university's real finance, procurement, ERP, logistics, or hardware systems. The real approval order, value limits, special-asset rules, required documents, and sustainability calculations must be confirmed with the instructors and relevant stakeholders.
